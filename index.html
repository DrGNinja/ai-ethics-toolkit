AI Ethics Faculty Toolkit

Five working tools from The Ethics of AI. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere. Use the tools in the session, keep them for the semester.

Ethical Decision Simulator

Pick a seat at the table. Each scenario has two decision points. Every choice shows its consequence — and which principle of the Lynn generative AI policy it honors or trades away. Presenting to a room? Turn on Room vote and tally hands before revealing anything.

Assignment Redesign Assistant

Paste one real assignment. You get an honest read on how exposed it is to a current model, a redesign using two of the eight patterns, syllabus language for the level you choose, and a rubric line that grades process. All analysis happens on this page.

AI Readiness Self-Assessment

Fifteen questions. Answer for your course, not for the institution — your course is the unit you control. Nothing is collected.

Detector Fallibility Demo

Three passages. Two minutes. Read each and decide whether a person or a model wrote it, then see what a detector would say.

Policy Language Library

Copy-and-paste language for your syllabus and assignments, taken from the Lynn University Policy on the Use of Generative AI and from working course practice. Nothing here needs to be written from scratch.

Reference frameworks

Four terms, one goal. AI Ethics asks what we should — and should not — do with AI. Responsible AI puts those principles into practice when building and using AI. AI Safety focuses on preventing serious or unintended harm. AI Governance is the policies, processes, rules, and structures behind responsible use. They overlap, but they are not the same thing.

Core principles of an AI code of ethics: fairness, transparency, explainability, accountability, privacy, safety and "do no harm," and sustainability — with final decisions kept in human hands.

External anchor: the "ETHICAL Principles AI Framework for Higher Education" by Wynants, Childers, De La Torre Roman, Budar-Turner, & Vasquez (2025), California State University, Fullerton — seven pillars: Exploration & Evaluation, Transparency & Accountability, Human-Centered Approach, Integrity & Academic Honesty, Continuous Learning & Innovation, Accessibility & Inclusivity, Legal & Ethical Compliance.
